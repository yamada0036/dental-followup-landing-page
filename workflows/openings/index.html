Openings workflow

Fill last-minute openings the same day.

A last-minute opening is only valuable on the day it appears. By tomorrow it is gone, and so is the production it could have held. Praxora makes openings visible the moment they appear, ranks the patients who could take them, and tracks the slot until it is filled or the day is over.

Where the leak happens

  • A cancellation is noted, but no one works the opening the same day.
  • The waitlist is a list of names with no order, so the front desk calls whoever comes to mind first.
  • Recall patients who could take the slot are never considered, because recall and openings live on separate lists.
  • The slot is only discovered as empty at the end of the day, when it is too late to fill.

An opening is not lost at the end of the day. It is lost in the first hours after it appears, when no one works it.

How Praxora works for openings

  1. Surface the opening the moment it appears A cancellation or a newly open slot becomes a visible case immediately, with the date, time, and length of the opening attached, so it cannot sit unnoticed.
  2. Rank who could take it Candidates are ranked from the waitlist and the recall list, so the front desk starts with the patients most likely to accept that slot, not with a random call list.
  3. Track the slot to an outcome Filled, offered and declined, or unfilled at the end of the day — every opening gets an outcome. Unfilled slots are counted, so the practice can see how much chair time is actually being lost.

That is the whole workflow: a case is captured, owned, worked to a recorded outcome, and only then closed. See also dental patient follow-up software and dental missed call recovery.

What you get

  • Same-day fills instead of end-of-day empty chairs.
  • A waitlist that is actually worked in order, instead of a list of names.
  • A monthly count of filled versus unfilled openings, so the owner can see the size of this leak.

Frequently asked questions

How fast does an opening need to be worked?

The same day it appears. An opening worked tomorrow is usually a lost slot — the value of this workflow is speed, not the number of names on a waitlist.

Where do candidates for an opening come from?

From the waitlist and from the recall list. Patients who are already overdue for hygiene, or already waiting for an earlier time, are often the fastest fills.

What if an opening cannot be filled?

Then it is recorded as unfilled, not ignored. Counting unfilled openings is how a practice learns whether this is a small leak or a large one — and whether confirmations need to improve upstream.
