Where the leak happens

  • During peak hours, when every line is busy and the front desk is checking patients in.
  • At lunch, when the phones are covered by whoever happens to be free — or by no one.
  • After hours, when a new-patient inquiry waits until the next morning, and often longer.
  • On the list itself: the call is written down, but no one owns it, so it is never worked to an outcome.

The cost is not the missed call. It is the case that never enters a workflow at all.

How Praxora works for missed calls

  1. Capture the case, not just the call Every missed or unanswered call becomes a case in the same workflow, with the reason for the call noted — new patient, existing patient, or treatment question — so the next person does not have to guess.
  2. Assign an owner and a next action Each case gets one named owner and one concrete next step, such as returning the call within one business day or offering two appointment times. Shared lists get re-pulled; owned cases get worked.
  3. Record the outcome and close the loop Booked, still thinking, unreachable, or needs a person — every outcome is recorded. A case with no outcome is still open, and it stays visible until someone closes it.

That is the whole workflow: a case is captured, owned, worked to a recorded outcome, and only then closed. See also dental patient follow-up software and dental missed call recovery.

What you get

  • A same-day view of every missed call that is still unresolved, instead of a voicemail pile.
  • No duplicate callbacks: the last touch and the next action are visible before anyone picks up the phone.
  • A weekly count the owner can actually use — calls recovered, calls still open, and calls that went cold.

Frequently asked questions

What counts as a missed call worth following up?

Any call that did not reach a person and did not end in a booked visit. New-patient inquiries go first, because they have no existing relationship to bring them back on their own.

How fast should a missed call be worked?

As an example only, many practices aim for the same business day for new-patient calls and within one business day for the rest. The exact window matters less than the rule: every case gets an owner and a due date the day it arrives.

Does this replace the front desk?

No. It makes the front desk's work smaller and clearer — a workable batch, a named owner, and a recorded outcome. Clinical questions, complaints, and sensitive replies still go to a person.
