The search is an appointment-recall question

If you searched for MaxAssist vs Dental Intelligence for appointment recall, you are usually trying to decide which system should get overdue hygiene and unscheduled patients back on the book. Both products are broader than that one job. Treating the comparison as “which logo has more features” hides the operational question: after a reminder goes out, who owns the patient who still has no appointment?

Descriptions below follow each company’s public pages as reviewed on 1 November 2026. Product screens change. Confirm the current workflow in a demo before you treat a sentence here as your contract. What MaxAssist publicly covers for recall

MaxAssist, formerly RecallMax, describes an onscreen assistant for the front desk. Its hygiene and recall page says the assistant is meant to show patients who still need personal attention after automated messages, and that it prioritizes hygiene patients using due dates, engagement history, and contact frequency. The same public feature set also describes unscheduled treatment, missed pre-appointed patients, and cancellation recovery.

That is a real recall workflow: a prioritized list, meant to work with automation rather than replace it, for people who did not book themselves. The longer evaluation questions are on MaxAssist alternatives for dental follow-up. RecallMax is the former name, not a second product: RecallMax alternatives.

What Dental Intelligence publicly covers for recall

Dental Intelligence is not only a dashboard. Its Follow Ups pages describe automated TouchPoints for repetitive outreach, including recare nudges and appointment reminders, with the practice choosing what the automation does. The same pages say that when automation is not enough, the work can be handed to a team member as a Team Task. Overdue hygiene is one of the examples: reminders go out, and patients who do not respond can be prioritized for a person.

Unscheduled treatment is described the same way: an automated touch first, then a task for cases that still need a person. Appointment confirmations and two-way texting are also part of that public Follow Ups story. A practice buying Dental Intelligence is often buying analytics, engagement, scheduling-related tools, and tasks together. This page does not claim those modules are identical to MaxAssist’s onscreen lists.

Where a two-product comparison usually stops

MaxAssist and Dental Intelligence can both put an overdue hygiene patient in front of a person. That overlap is why the search exists. The useful difference is how the team sees the next action: an onscreen list organized for the front desk, or an automated sequence that escalates to a task.

Neither description, by itself, answers a smaller question. A patient who said the crown can wait until next month, or who needs to talk with a spouse about the fee, is not the same follow-up as a patient who simply missed a reminder. If that reason lives only in a sticky note, both platforms can still leave the front desk guessing. Ask to see one real overdue recall patient and one unscheduled-treatment patient, including the owner and the outcome, before you decide the category is settled.

Reminder activity and a recovered appointment are different measurements. The reminder side of that line is recall lists versus recall recovery. The problem of an open case with no next step is follow-up leakage.

Praxora as a third option

Praxora is a follow-up recovery workflow, not a third all-in-one platform. It is built to turn known leaks into a daily front-desk queue: missed calls, unscheduled treatment, and overdue recall. Each open case is meant to show a current state, a reason follow-up stopped, a next action, one owner, and a recorded outcome. Human review stays in the path.

Use it as the comparison when the practice already has MaxAssist, Dental Intelligence, or another engagement tool and still cannot see which unresolved patients the front desk should work today. Chairside intent is a different input

Appointment recall often starts from a due date. A lot of unscheduled treatment starts earlier, in the chair, when the patient states a reason and leaves without a booking. Typical reasons are ordinary and operational: the fee is a concern, they want to talk with a spouse, or they ask to decide next month.

Praxora is designed to carry that reason into the queue the front desk actually works, instead of dropping the patient onto a generic recall campaign. Staff approve the message. The product is meant to support the team that already knows the patient. It is not an answering agent and it does not diagnose. The same boundary is why a missed call is its own case, not only an automatic text: missed-call text-back vs recovery.

When to stay with the platform you have

Stay with MaxAssist when the team wants that broader engagement suite and the onscreen recall list is already how the front desk chooses the next patient. Stay with Dental Intelligence when the practice wants analytics, Follow Ups, and Team Tasks in one performance platform and the gap is how the team uses tasks, not a missing category.

Switching products does not create an owner. If every overdue patient already has a current state, a next date, and a named person, another layer is optional. A focused queue is worth a look when those fields are blank after the standard reminders have already run. What to ask in a demo

  • Show one overdue hygiene patient who received the standard reminders and still has no appointment. Who owns the next action, and what is the date?
  • Show one patient who said they need to talk with a spouse, and one who asked to wait until next month. Do they get the same message?
  • Show one unscheduled-treatment case that was contacted and did not book. Is the outcome “message sent” or a recorded next step?
  • What is automatic, and what does a person approve before it goes out?
