What Is Follow-Up Leakage in a Dental Practice?

Follow-up leakage happens when a patient opportunity falls out of the normal follow-up process without a clear next action, owner, or tracked outcome.

What Is Follow-Up Leakage?

Follow-up leakage is an operational gap. It is not simply a missed call, an overdue patient, or an unscheduled treatment plan. It is what happens when that opportunity does not move into a reliable process.

Why Follow-Up Leakage Is an Operational Problem

More leads do not fix broken follow-up. If patient intent already exists but the next action is unclear, the practice may spend more on acquisition while existing opportunities remain unowned.

Missed Calls: When Recovery Never Starts

A missed call should be found, classified, prioritized, assigned, followed up, and tracked. Learn how to build a missed call recovery workflow.

Unscheduled Treatment: When Follow-Up Stalls

The patient already exists and the treatment recommendation already exists. The leak starts when the patient leaves without a scheduled next step. Read about unscheduled treatment follow-up.

Overdue Recall: When a List Replaces a Workflow

A recall list identifies who is overdue. A workflow defines who owns the next action and what outcome is tracked. Compare recall lists vs recall recovery workflows.

Why More Leads Do Not Fix Broken Follow-Up

If follow-up leakage is high, new acquisition can simply add more opportunities to the same broken process. Practices should measure leakage before assuming the answer is more demand.

The Follow-Up Recovery Framework

  • Find
  • Classify
  • Prioritize
  • Human Review
  • Follow Up
  • Recover

What Practice Owners Should Measure

  • Number of opportunities in each workflow.
  • Whether each opportunity has an owner.
  • How quickly the first follow-up happens.
  • Whether replies, bookings, deferrals, and closed outcomes are tracked.
