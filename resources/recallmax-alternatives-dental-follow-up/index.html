RecallMax is now MaxAssist

MaxAssist currently identifies itself as formerly RecallMax. The product has expanded well beyond recall into a broader dental productivity and patient engagement platform with scheduling, unscheduled treatment, hygiene and recall, missed pre-appointed patients, cancellation recovery, analytics, communication, and AI-powered workflow features.

That means a search for “RecallMax alternatives” can describe several different buying needs. One practice may want a better recall workflow. Another may want unscheduled treatment prioritization. Another may be looking for a broader front desk platform.

Before comparing products, define the operational problem.

Are you trying to improve recall or all follow-up workflows?

If the problem is specifically overdue hygiene and recall, the comparison should focus on how the tool identifies overdue patients, keeps the current state accurate, handles previous outreach, shows the next action, and records outcomes.

If the problem also includes unscheduled treatment, missed pre-appointed patients, cancellations, or other patient follow-up, the practice may need a broader workflow layer.

Start by separating three questions:

  • Which patient opportunity sources matter?
  • What context does the team need before follow-up?
  • What outcome should be visible after follow-up?

Compare a recall list with a recall recovery workflow

A recall list tells the practice who is overdue. A recall recovery workflow tells the team what happens next.

When evaluating RecallMax or MaxAssist alternatives, ask whether staff can see:

  • The patient's current state
  • Whether the patient was recently contacted
  • Whether the patient asked to be contacted later
  • Whether an appointment is already scheduled
  • The next action
  • The owner
  • The current outcome

Compare unscheduled treatment follow-up separately

Unscheduled treatment is not simply another recall sequence. The treatment opportunity already exists, and different patients may have different reasons for not scheduling.

Operational classifications may include financial hesitation, timing, a need for more information, a request to decide later, inability to reach the patient, or an unknown follow-up status.

A useful treatment follow-up workflow should help the team understand why the patient stopped moving before applying the same outreach to everyone.

See the guide to unscheduled treatment follow-up without overloading the front desk.

Compare how much history the team must reconstruct

When a front desk employee opens an opportunity, how quickly can they understand the relevant context?

Ask whether the team can see:

  • Current state
  • Why follow-up stopped
  • Previous relevant touch
  • Next action
  • Owner
  • Status

The goal is not to remove notes. Notes remain important history. The goal is to prevent the team from rebuilding the current state from a long sequence of notes before every follow-up attempt.

Compare prioritization and explainability

A ranked list can be useful, especially when the practice has more open opportunities than staff can contact in one day. But the team should understand what the priority represents.

When evaluating alternatives, ask:

  • What information influences the order of the queue?
  • Can staff see why an opportunity needs attention?
  • Can a person review or override the action?
  • How are incomplete or unknown patient states handled?

Praxora's positioning is not “let AI make every follow-up decision.” The workflow concept is classify, prioritize, and keep human review visible before follow-up where context matters.

Compare outcome tracking

A recall or treatment workflow should not stop at “message sent” or “task completed.” The practice should be able to see whether the opportunity remains open.

Possible operational outcomes include booked, reached but not ready, asked to contact later, unable to reach, already resolved, or closed. These are examples rather than required industry standards.

The broader recovery funnel can be viewed as Found → Prioritized → Approved → Contacted → Replied → Booked → Recovered.

Which type of alternative should you evaluate?

Evaluate a broad dental productivity platform when the practice wants scheduling, patient communication, analytics, multiple patient lists, and other front desk functions in one ecosystem.

Evaluate a focused recall or reminder tool when the main problem is consistent recall communication.

Evaluate a focused follow-up recovery layer when the practice already has patient opportunities but the current state, reason follow-up stopped, priority, next action, ownership, or outcome is unclear.

Where should a practice start?

Do not start with a brand comparison spreadsheet. Start with one open workflow and identify where the team loses clarity.
