What this report shows

Use the list to ask what care is still planned, whether the relevant visit is booked, and what the patient actually agreed to. A row cannot answer all three questions by itself. In particular, an appointment for one type of visit does not establish that all other planned care has been scheduled.

On its own, the report is just a list. It becomes a workflow when every row carries three things: a named owner responsible for the next step, a next action with a date, and a recorded outcome once the case moves. Without those, the same report gets re-run for months while the same cases sit untouched.

Pulling the report in the Treatment Finder

Open Standard Reports → Lists → Treatment Finder. The initial list targets active patients with fee-bearing planned care, no appointment and calendar-year insurance. To adapt it:

  1. Leave Include patients with upcoming appointments unchecked for an unscheduled-patient list.
  2. Set TP Date From / To, your clinic and provider. Use Code Range for a focused procedure group.
  3. Review insurance filters. Include secondary insurance and no insurance broadens coverage and overrides the benefit-start-month filter.
  4. Click Refresh List, review the result, then use Export to File for a text or Excel file.

Check the official Open Dental Treatment Finder manual and screenshots for your version. This is an independent guide, not an Open Dental integration or endorsement.

Prioritizing the export

A long export needs a review order. First honor patient preferences, agreed contact dates and the clinical team’s instructions. Within eligible cases, consider:

  1. Expiry first. Cases with insurance benefits that expire or reset soon go first — that coverage disappears whether or not the patient acts.
  2. Value next. Among cases with similar expiry, work higher-value treatment first.
  3. Days since last contact. Ties break toward the cases that have sat longest without a touch, so nothing goes months without attention.

These are operational sorting choices, not clinical urgency scores. Confirm benefit dates and coverage before referring to them, and avoid implying that a financial deadline changes a patient’s clinical need.

Owners and recorded outcomes

Every row on the worked list needs an owner — one named person responsible for the next action — plus a next action with a date and a recorded outcome when the case moves: scheduled, declined, waiting on insurance, unreachable, and so on.

Before any outreach, a person reviews the row and confirms the case is still open. Reports are snapshots; patients schedule, complete, or decline treatment between exports. Human review of the current state is what keeps the team from contacting the wrong patients, and it is a non-negotiable step in the workflow.

Starting with your first 20

Choose a batch your team can finish, such as twenty open cases. For each one, record the source date, current appointment check, owner, known barrier, next action and follow-up date. Keep the working file inside your practice’s approved systems; the public calculator and free review only need aggregate counts and values.

Before adding the next batch, reconcile replies and appointments with the practice record. Count bookings separately from completed care and collections. A patient who asks to wait needs an agreed next date; a declined plan needs an honest closed status. If two team members could contact the same person, choose one owner before outreach. These habits make the process workable even before any automation is configured.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the unscheduled treatment report in Open Dental?

Open Standard Reports, then Lists, then Treatment Finder. Review the filters, refresh the list and export it. Verify acceptance and current appointment status before outreach.

How often should our practice pull this report?

Most practices do well pulling it weekly. Have a person review each row before outreach, because reports go stale: patients schedule, complete, or decline treatment between exports.

What do we do with cases that have been unscheduled for months?

Re-check that the treatment is still open and appropriate, then update the owner and the next action and record the outcome honestly — including declined or not a candidate. A short list of real opportunities beats a long list of dead rows.
