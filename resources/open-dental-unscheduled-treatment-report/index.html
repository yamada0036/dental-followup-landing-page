How to pull the Open Dental unscheduled treatment report

The unscheduled treatment report is where diagnosed, presented treatment sits when it never gets scheduled. Here is how to pull it in Open Dental, prioritize the export, and work every case to a recorded outcome.

What this report shows

The report answers three questions about every row: what was diagnosed, what was presented to the patient, and what has no appointment on the books. That is the entire definition of unscheduled treatment: the plan exists, the patient has heard about it, and nothing is scheduled.

On its own, the report is just a list. It becomes a workflow when every row carries three things: a named owner responsible for the next step, a next action with a date, and a recorded outcome once the case moves. Without those, the same report gets re-run for months while the same cases sit untouched.

Pulling the report in the Treatment Finder

The Treatment Finder is built to find treatment plans and planned procedures by status, provider, procedure code, and insurance. To build your unscheduled treatment list:

  1. Open the Treatment Finder from the reports area of Open Dental.
  2. Filter for planned procedures with no scheduled appointment — diagnosed and presented treatment that has nothing on the books.
  3. Narrow by remaining insurance benefits when you want to work expiring coverage first.
  4. Filter by procedure code when you want to work one high-value treatment type at a time, such as crowns.
  5. Export the filtered list to a spreadsheet so the front desk works a named, owned list instead of glancing at a report.

Open Dental's menu names and report options can change slightly between versions. If a label above does not match your screen, look for the nearest equivalent: the pattern — planned, not scheduled, filterable, exportable — stays the same.

Prioritizing the export

A long export is not a plan. Work the list in this order:

  1. Expiry first. Cases with insurance benefits that expire or reset soon go first — that coverage disappears whether or not the patient acts.
  2. Value next. Among cases with similar expiry, work higher-value treatment first.
  3. Days since last contact. Ties break toward the cases that have sat longest without a touch, so nothing goes months without attention.

Do not work the list alphabetically, and do not work whoever the front desk happens to remember. Priority by expiry, then value, then waiting time is the whole system.

Owners and recorded outcomes

Every row on the worked list needs an owner — one named person responsible for the next action — plus a next action with a date and a recorded outcome when the case moves: scheduled, declined, waiting on insurance, unreachable, and so on.

Before any outreach, a person reviews the row and confirms the case is still open. Reports are snapshots; patients schedule, complete, or decline treatment between exports. Human review of the current state is what keeps the team from contacting the wrong patients, and it is a non-negotiable step in the workflow.

Starting with your first 20

Do not try to work three hundred rows at once. Take the top twenty by the priority rule above, and work them until each one has a recorded outcome. Then pull the next twenty. Count two numbers: how many cases scheduled, and how much treatment value moved. A small list worked to completion beats a large report that everyone glances at and nobody owns.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I find the unscheduled treatment report in Open Dental?

Use the Treatment Finder to list planned procedures that have not been scheduled, then filter by insurance remaining or procedure code and export the list. Menu names can vary slightly between Open Dental versions, so match the steps to the labels in your own install.

How often should our practice pull this report?

Most practices do well pulling it weekly. Have a person review each row before outreach, because reports go stale: patients schedule, complete, or decline treatment between exports.

What do we do with cases that have been unscheduled for months?

Re-check that the treatment is still open and appropriate, then update the owner and the next action and record the outcome honestly — including declined or not a candidate. A short list of real opportunities beats a long list of dead rows.
