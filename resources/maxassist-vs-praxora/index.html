RecallMax and MaxAssist are one product line

MaxAssist’s own site says it was formerly RecallMax. If you are comparing “RecallMax alternatives” and “MaxAssist alternatives,” you are looking at the same company under the current name. The longer evaluation checklist is on MaxAssist alternatives for dental follow-up. The naming note is on RecallMax alternatives.

This page does not claim Praxora replaces that platform. It compares a broad engagement suite with a focused recovery layer for three leaks: missed calls, unscheduled treatment, and overdue recall.

Where MaxAssist is the broader fit

Stay with MaxAssist, or evaluate it first, when the practice wants one vendor for several front-desk jobs at once: prioritized patient lists, two-way texting, reminders, online scheduling, digital forms, review requests, cancellation fill-in, and multi-location analytics. That breadth is real. A follow-up recovery tool that refuses those jobs is not a substitute for them.

Also stay if the team already trusts the daily action plan and the gap is training or ownership inside MaxAssist, not a missing category. Switching products does not create an owner.

Where Praxora is the narrower fit

Evaluate Praxora when the practice already has communication tools and still cannot answer five questions about an open case: what is true now, why follow-up stopped, what happens next, who owns it, and what outcome was recorded. That is the problem described as follow-up leakage.

It is also the better first conversation when the buyer’s objection is integration. You can estimate the three leaks and review the workflow before anyone approves a Dentrix or Open Dental connection. Praxora does not diagnose, and it does not send clinical advice. Human review stays in the path when a reply needs a person.

How to compare price

Do not compare a MaxAssist starting rate with a guessed Praxora price. This site does not publish one. On 31 October 2026, MaxAssist’s plans page listed Pro starting rates of $395, $445, and $495 per month, and Platinum starting rates of $495, $545, and $595 per month, across the bands on that page. The solo-practice block showed Pro from $495 and Platinum from $595. The page says final pricing depends on locations and active patients, and Enterprise is a quote. Optional add-ons were listed from $49.99 per month.

Those are their published starting rates, not your invoice, and not an estimate invented for this page. The sourced table, including Weave’s published “from $199 per month” and the note that Dental Intelligence’s practice price was not on a public list, is in published price bands.

The economic test that does not require their price or ours: how many completed appointments would have to come from cases that are open today? Put your own counts into the calculator before you sit through two demos.

What to ask in a demo

  • Show one unscheduled-treatment case that was contacted, booked something else, and should still be open. What does the screen say?
  • Show one missed call from yesterday that is still unresolved. Who owns it, and what is the next action?
  • Show an overdue recall patient who asked to wait. Where is the next date?
  • What is written back to Dentrix or Open Dental, and what still requires a person?
  • What is the first-year total, including messaging, locations, and add-ons, in writing?

Frequently asked questions

Is MaxAssist the same product as RecallMax?

MaxAssist says it was formerly RecallMax. Compare them as one product line, not as two competing tools.

Does MaxAssist already follow up unscheduled treatment?

Yes. MaxAssist publicly describes unscheduled treatment, hygiene and recall, missed pre-appointed patients, cancellation recovery, reminders, two-way texting, and related front-desk workflows. The useful question is whether your team can see the owner, the next action, and the outcome.

Is Praxora a MaxAssist replacement?

No. Praxora does not claim to replace phones, forms, reviews, online scheduling, or a full engagement suite. It is a follow-up recovery workflow for missed calls, unscheduled treatment, and overdue recall, and it can be evaluated before a practice-management integration.
