Start with an honest view of what Lighthouse 360 already does

Henry Schein One describes Lighthouse 360 as patient communication that automates reminders and confirmations, asks for reviews, sends recall reminders, and supports scheduling and pre-visit forms. That is a communication and patient-experience product. A practice shopping for it is often trying to cut no-shows and stop building every reminder by hand.

Public pages, reviewed on 1 November 2026, describe a dental communication product. Henry Schein One’s product page emphasizes automated reminders and confirmations by text, email, and phone, review and feedback requests, recall reminders, and communication that stays tied to the schedule. Lighthouse’s own features pages add continuing-care reminders when a patient is due for a cleaning, reminders for dormant patients, procedure-based messages, and a cancellation fill-in text. Two-way texting and front-desk task lists are described on Lighthouse 360+. A separate public page describes treatment-plan reminders that can stop when treatment is scheduled.

Those are the vendor’s descriptions, not a measured result for any practice. This page does not repeat percentage claims from marketing articles. Confirm which items are in the edition a practice would buy. Lighthouse 360 and Demandforce are different products even where both have been discussed alongside Henry Schein. Demandforce is covered on Demandforce alternatives.

The more useful comparison is operational: how quickly can the team understand the current state, why follow-up stopped, what should happen next, who owns the next action, and what outcome was recorded?

Why “alternative” searches are usually workflow questions

Practices rarely search for software alternatives because they suddenly want a different logo. They usually have a workflow requirement, a usability concern, a budget constraint, a change in practice size, or a gap between the current system and the way the team actually works.

Follow-up recovery assumes the opportunity is already known. A call was missed. Treatment was discussed and not booked. A recall patient is overdue after the usual reminders. The next question is operational: why did it stop, who owns it, and what outcome was recorded? That problem is follow-up leakage. The same category line, for a different communication platform, is Weave vs follow-up recovery.

For dental follow-up, the evaluation question may sound like:

  • Can the front desk see who needs attention first?
  • Can staff understand why the patient stopped moving without reading a long history?
  • Is the current patient state still valid?
  • Is the next action visible?
  • Can one person clearly own the next step?
  • Can the practice see the outcome after outreach?

Those questions create a better software comparison than a generic checklist of reminders, texting, dashboards, and reports.

Compare the opportunity source

First ask what kinds of patient opportunities the system is designed to surface.

A dental practice may need help with:

  • Unscheduled restorative treatment
  • Overdue hygiene and recall
  • Missed pre-appointed patients
  • Cancellation recovery
  • Missed calls
  • Other patient follow-up workflows

Lighthouse 360 publicly covers several of these categories — reminders, confirmations, recall reminders, review requests, pre-visit forms, and treatment-plan reminders. An alternative should be evaluated against the specific workflow the practice is trying to improve. A narrow product may be a better fit for one workflow; a broader platform may be a better fit for practices trying to consolidate multiple front desk functions.

Compare how the current patient state is validated

A follow-up queue is only useful when the state behind the queue is still true. Before staff contact a patient, the workflow should help answer whether the patient is already scheduled, already contacted, deferred to a later date, closed, or otherwise no longer an open opportunity.

This matters because stale lists create unnecessary work and increase the risk of duplicate or poorly timed outreach.

When comparing tools, ask:

  • What data determines whether the opportunity is still open?
  • How quickly does a changed appointment or treatment state update the queue?
  • Can staff see the current state without opening multiple screens?
  • How are unresolved or unknown states handled?

Praxora treats current-state review as an operational check inside the recovery workflow. The public framework remains Find → Classify → Prioritize → Human Review → Follow Up → Recover.

Compare how the system explains why follow-up stopped

Priority is easier to understand when the team also knows the follow-up context. An opportunity may be open because of timing, financial hesitation, a need for more information, a request to decide later, inability to reach the patient, or an unknown status.

These are operational examples rather than universal categories. The important question is whether the tool gives staff enough context to choose the next action.

A recovery queue should not require the front desk to read a novel of notes before deciding what happens next. A treatment-plan reminder that stops once treatment is scheduled is a communication rule — it does not, by itself, record why the patient hesitated. That gap is covered in Treatment-plan reminders are another boundary below.

When comparing software, ask whether staff can quickly see:

  • Why the patient remains open in the workflow
  • The relevant previous touch
  • The current state
  • The next action to review

Compare prioritization without treating the score as magic

Prioritized patient lists are valuable when they reduce guesswork. But every practice should understand what the prioritization is trying to optimize.

Lighthouse 360’s public pages describe continuing-care and dormant-patient reminders ordered by due date, not a follow-up priority score that weighs treatment value, the reason follow-up stopped, and reachability together. A reminder list ordered by due date is not the same as a worked queue ordered by what needs a person first.

Ask:

  • What information influences priority?
  • Can staff understand why an opportunity is high priority?
  • Can the practice review or override the next action?
  • Does the system separate “high value” from “needs attention now”?
  • How are incomplete or unknown states handled?

Praxora does not present a clinically validated universal patient priority score. The product concept is to organize operational context and make human review visible where judgment matters.

Compare the next action, not only the patient list

A ranked list still leaves an important question: what does the team do with the first patient on the list?

A useful next-action view may show:

  • Current state: Treatment discussed, not scheduled
  • Why follow-up stopped: Financial hesitation
  • Last touch: June 22
  • Priority: High
  • Recommended next action: Human-reviewed financing follow-up
  • Owner: Treatment Coordinator
  • Status: Ready for Review

Simulated workflow example. Demo data. This is not a customer case study or a recovery result.

The software comparison should ask whether the queue helps the team act, not only whether it helps the team sort. Front-desk task lists inside a communication product can show missing forms, birthdays, or other prep. A recovery queue should show a different set of fields: current state, why follow-up stopped, last relevant touch, next action, owner, and outcome. Ask which screen the team opens at the start of the day when the work is “patients who did not book.”

If that answer is already clear inside Lighthouse 360, you may not need another layer. If the answer is a spreadsheet next to the reminder history, the layer you are missing is ownership, not another broadcast. Activity and outcome are different counts. Praxora’s working stages are found, prioritized, approved, contacted, replied, booked, and recovered. “Approved” is there because a person reviews the next message.

Compare ownership and human review

Dental follow-up can involve sensitive financial context, patient confusion, clinical questions, or complex treatment decisions. A product that generates an action should also make it clear when a person needs to review that action.

Ask:

  • Can an opportunity have a visible owner?
  • Can the next action be reviewed before outreach?
  • Can staff route clinical questions to the appropriate person?
  • Can the team see whether the action is approved, pending, or completed?

This is why Human Review remains an explicit stage in Praxora's recovery framework.

Compare outcomes, not only activity

Texts sent, calls made, and tasks completed are useful activity metrics. A follow-up recovery workflow should also make outcomes visible.

Praxora uses a recovery funnel concept:

  • Found
  • Prioritized
  • Approved
  • Contacted
  • Replied
  • Booked
  • Recovered

When comparing products, ask whether the practice can see where the workflow stalls. Are opportunities being found but never prioritized? Are tasks completed without patient response? Are patients replying without a booked outcome?

Praxora is built to turn known leaks into one daily queue for the front desk:

  • A missed call that still has no owner after the first text or voicemail.
  • Unscheduled treatment, including a chairside reason such as price, a spouse, or next month.
  • Overdue recall that remains open after the standard reminders.

Staff approve messages. The workflow is meant to support the team, not replace the front desk or the treatment coordinator. Praxora is early. It does not publish customer stories or a subscription price. It does not replace reminders, forms, or review software, and it does not diagnose.

The recall half of that list is recall leakage. The treatment half is unscheduled treatment follow-up. The call half is missed call recovery.

It may also be the better fit when the practice is already standardizing on that vendor’s other systems and wants patient messaging in the same family. Evaluate a recovery layer when reminders are already running and the unresolved work is still informal. The front desk cannot see, in one place, which missed call, which unscheduled plan, and which overdue recall patient needs a person today. Priority is a guess. Outcomes are “we sent something.”

Reminders are one boundary

Lighthouse 360’s recall and confirmation tools are designed to contact patients who are due, overdue, or already on the book. A confirmation that lands in the practice-management system saves a manual update. That is useful, and it is not the same as a recovery case.

The recovery question begins when the patient does not book or does not confirm:

  • Is the current recall status still true?
  • Did someone already call?
  • Is there a next date, or only another reminder?
  • Who owns the row this week?

See dental recall software vs recall recovery for that distinction without a vendor name attached.

Treatment-plan reminders are another boundary

Lighthouse publicly describes treatment-plan reminders aimed at patients with unscheduled treatment, including messages that can stop once treatment is scheduled. That is a communication rule. It does not, by itself, record why the patient hesitated.

A patient who said the fee was the problem should not automatically get the same next step as a patient who asked to decide next month or to talk with a spouse. Praxora is designed to keep that chairside intent on the case the front desk works. How those reasons change the action is on treatment follow-up by patient intent. Message wording the team can edit is on unscheduled treatment text samples.

Can they work together?

Conceptually, yes. Lighthouse 360 can keep sending reminders, confirmations, and recall messages. A recovery workflow can hold the cases that remain open and record what a person did next. This page does not claim that integration exists.

Frequently asked questions

What is Lighthouse 360 designed to do?

Henry Schein One describes Lighthouse 360 as dental patient communication: automated reminders and confirmations, recall reminders, review requests, and pre-visit forms. Its own features pages also describe continuing-care outreach, dormant-patient reminders, treatment-plan reminders, and two-way texting on Lighthouse 360+.

Does follow-up recovery replace Lighthouse 360?

No. A recovery workflow does not replace reminders, confirmations, forms, or review requests. Praxora is an early product built for unresolved missed calls, unscheduled treatment, and overdue recall, with staff approving messages.

Can Lighthouse 360 and a recovery queue coexist?

Conceptually, yes. Communication software can send the reminders while a recovery queue tracks which opportunities are still open. This page does not claim a live Praxora integration with Lighthouse 360.
