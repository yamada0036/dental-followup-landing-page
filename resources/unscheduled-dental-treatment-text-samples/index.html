1. When cost is the obstacle

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. Would a short call to review the options we discussed be helpful? Reply YES and our team will follow up, or call [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Use this when a patient asked about cost. Offer a conversation about verified options without promising financing approval.

2. When time is the obstacle

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. Would you like help finding an appointment that fits your schedule? Reply with a good time for us to call, or call [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Ask about availability first. Confirm real appointment openings before offering a specific slot; do not imply that a place has been held.

3. When the patient is still thinking

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. Just checking whether you have any questions after our last conversation. Would you like a call from our team? Reply YES or call [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Reopen the conversation without assuming the patient accepted treatment. Route clinical questions to the care team.

4. When it is a family decision

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. You mentioned wanting time to discuss your options. Would you like us to check back on a date that works for you? Reply with a preferred date, or call [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Use this only when the patient actually asked for decision time. Agree on a follow-up date instead of inventing an estimate deadline.

5. When a benefits review may help

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. Would you like our team to review your benefit questions before you choose an appointment? Reply YES or call [Phone]. Benefits and coverage need confirmation. Reply STOP to opt out.

Confirm the patient's plan and benefit dates before discussing expiry. This message does not promise coverage, reimbursement or savings.

Texting compliance basics

Before your team sends any of these, review the message in the context of your practice’s policies and the patient’s preferences:

  • Consent first. Only text patients who have agreed to receive texts from your practice.
  • Keep the message general. These templates avoid procedure names. Use an approved private channel for treatment, insurance or financial details.
  • Opt-out in every message. Each sample above ends with a clear opt-out — keep it there, and honor every STOP immediately.
  • Human review before sending. A person confirms the case is still open — still unscheduled, still appropriate — before any message goes out.

From templates to a workflow

Templates start conversations. Follow-up is what recovers treatment. When a patient replies, someone needs to read the reply, update the owner and the next action, and record the outcome — scheduled, declined, waiting on insurance, needs a call. Without that step, even the best template is just another sent message nobody tracks.

Frequently asked questions

Can our practice text patients about unscheduled treatment?

Yes — but only text patients who have agreed to receive texts from your practice. Keep messages short, never include sensitive health details, and include a clear opt-out in every message. Have a person confirm the case is still open before sending.

How long should a follow-up text be?

Keep it short: one idea and one ask per message. If the conversation needs more detail, the text should earn a phone call, not try to replace it.

What should we do when a patient replies?

A person reads the reply, updates the owner and the next action, and records the outcome — scheduled, declined, waiting, and so on. The template starts the conversation; the recorded outcome is what turns outreach into a real follow-up system.
