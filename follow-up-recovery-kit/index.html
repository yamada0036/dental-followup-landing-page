1. What Is Follow-Up Leakage?

Follow-up leakage happens when a patient opportunity falls out of the normal follow-up process without a clear next action, owner, or tracked outcome.

This kit covers three common examples: missed calls, unscheduled treatment, and overdue recall.

The goal is not to automate every patient interaction. The goal is to make the recovery process visible, reviewable, and measurable.

2. The Follow-Up Recovery Framework

Find
Identify opportunities that may have fallen out of the normal process.

Classify
Understand where follow-up stopped and why.

Prioritize
Decide which opportunities need attention first.

Human Review
Keep staff judgment in the workflow when context or sensitivity matters.

Follow Up
Move the opportunity into a clear next action.

Recover
Track replies, bookings, and outcomes.

3. Missed Call Recovery Workflow

A missed call is only the first event. Follow-up leakage begins when the missed call never enters a clear recovery process.

  1. Find the missed call.
  2. Identify whether the caller appears to be an existing patient, new patient, or unknown.
  3. Classify the likely context when information is available.
  4. Prioritize calls where a patient opportunity may require timely attention.
  5. Assign an owner or review queue.
  6. Follow up using the appropriate channel.
  7. Track the outcome: reached, replied, booked, no response, or not a patient opportunity.

4. Unscheduled Treatment Follow-Up Workflow

The patient already exists. The treatment recommendation already exists. The workflow may have stalled after the patient left without scheduling.

  1. Find open treatment plans.
  2. Identify when the treatment opportunity became unscheduled.
  3. Classify the likely follow-up status, such as financial hesitation, timing, needs more information, patient asked to decide later, unable to reach, or status unknown.
  4. Prioritize by timing, workflow status, and estimated opportunity.
  5. Review the next recommended action.
  6. Follow up.
  7. Track replies, information requests, scheduling support requests, bookings, deferrals, and no responses.

This workflow should not infer clinical diagnoses. It supports follow-up on treatment already presented by the practice.

5. Overdue Recall Reactivation Workflow

A recall list tells the practice who is overdue. A recovery workflow defines what happens next.

  1. Find overdue recall patients.
  2. Segment by how long they have been overdue.
  3. Identify previous follow-up status when available.
  4. Prioritize reactivation opportunities.
  5. Review outreach.
  6. Follow up.
  7. Track reached, replied, reactivated, booked, deferred, and no response outcomes.

6. A Simple 30-Day Recovery Plan

Week 1 - Find

Choose one workflow. Do not start with missed calls, unscheduled treatment, and overdue recall at the same time. Pull the existing opportunity list and understand its size.

Week 2 - Classify and Prioritize

Review why follow-up may have stopped. Define a small number of operational categories. Prioritize the opportunities that require attention first.

Week 3 - Review and Follow Up

Create a clear review process. Assign ownership. Begin follow-up with a controlled group.

Week 4 - Measure Outcomes

Measure found, prioritized, approved, contacted, replied, booked, and recovered. Then review where the workflow still breaks.

7. Follow-Up Leakage Checklist

  • [ ] We know how missed calls are identified.
  • [ ] Missed calls have a clear owner or review queue.
  • [ ] We can identify unscheduled treatment opportunities.
  • [ ] We know why treatment follow-up may have stopped.
  • [ ] We prioritize instead of contacting every patient the same way.
  • [ ] Human review is used when context or sensitivity matters.
  • [ ] Overdue recall patients move through a defined process.
  • [ ] We track replies and bookings.
  • [ ] We can identify where follow-up stops.
  • [ ] We review recovery outcomes regularly.
