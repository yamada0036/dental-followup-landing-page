Five copy-ready recall messages

Replace [First name], [Practice] and [Phone]. These examples do not name a diagnosis or procedure. Review the wording and communication rules with your practice before sending.

1. Routine check-in when a visit is due

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. Would you like help finding a time for your next visit? Reply with a good time to call, or reach us at [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Use only after confirming that a return visit is due and no appointment is already booked.

2. Patient asked to be contacted later

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. You asked us to check back around this time. Would you like us to help with an appointment, or would another date work better? Call [Phone] or reply here. Reply STOP to opt out.

Send on the date the patient actually requested; do not invent a prior conversation.

3. Scheduling was inconvenient

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. If scheduling has been difficult, our team can help find a time that works. Would you prefer a call or a text with available options? [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Confirm real openings before offering times; route the reply to someone who can book.

4. Gentle reactivation after a longer gap

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. We would be happy to see you again when the timing is right. Would you like our team to help plan a visit? Reply YES or call [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Avoid claiming urgency or overdue status in the message; review the current record first.

5. Close the loop without repeated nudges

Hi [First name], this is [Practice]. We will pause our check-ins for now. If you would like help scheduling a visit later, reply here or call [Phone]. Reply STOP to opt out.

Use only when your practice has decided to pause this outreach; update the follow-up status so the next campaign does not restart it.

Before sending any recall text

  • Confirm the patient is still due and has not booked elsewhere in the practice.
  • Check their communication preferences and your practice's messaging policy.
  • Keep treatment details and sensitive information out of a generic text.
  • Have a person handle replies, opt-outs and questions that need clinical review.
